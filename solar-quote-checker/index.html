    How the check works

    1. Price per watt: your total price divided by your total panel watts. A complete on-grid system in Pakistan (panels + inverter + structure + installation, no battery) cost about Rs 78–123 per watt in late November 2026 — a 5 kW system was Rs 3.96–5.79 lakh and a 10 kW system Rs 7.79–12.23 lakh.
    2. Battery adder: for a hybrid system, lithium storage costs about Rs 45,000–65,000 per kWh installed (most quotes near Rs 52,000/kWh) — a 5 kWh bank about Rs 2.62 lakh, a 10 kWh bank about Rs 5.26 lakh. That is added to the fair range above.
    3. Panels: Tier-1 A-grade panels sold for about Rs 38–45 per watt (typical Rs 40/W) in November 2026. Cheaper panels exist, but they are usually lower-grade or lesser-known brands.
    4. Panel-to-inverter ratio: total panel kW divided by inverter kW. 1.0–1.3 is normal — a little more panel than inverter is good. Above 1.4 the inverter clips power on sunny days; below 0.9 you paid for inverter capacity you will not use.
    5. Battery usable capacity: a battery never gives you its full label size. Lithium (LiFePO4) gives about 90% usable, tubular lead-acid only about 50% — so a 5 kWh lithium bank gives ~4.5 kWh of backup, while a 5 kWh tubular bank gives only ~2.5 kWh.

    Questions

    My quote is much cheaper than the fair range. Is that good?

    Not always. A very cheap quote often means B-grade or undocumented panels, a no-name inverter, a thin structure, or items left out (net billing application, earthing, wiring). Ask the installer to put the panel brand, model and grade in writing, and compare two more quotes before you decide.

    On-grid or hybrid — which should I pick?

    On-grid is cheaper and fine if you only want lower bills. Hybrid costs much more because of the battery, but it keeps your fans, lights and fridge running during load-shedding. Pick hybrid only if you need backup power — and size the battery from your backup load, not from the panel size.

    Why does net billing matter for my quote?

    Since February 2026, new solar connections in Pakistan are on net billing, not net metering: exported units earn only about Rs 8–11 each instead of one-for-one. So a bigger system does not pay back the way it used to — size the system for the electricity you use in the day, not for maximum export.

    What should be in writing before I pay anything?

    Panel brand, model, wattage and grade; inverter brand, model and warranty; battery brand, capacity, warranty and cycle life; structure type; what installation includes; and who applies for net billing. If it is not written down, it is not promised.
